# Cross-fitting for the R-learner

The R-learner estimates marginal outcome probability m(X) on other training folds and uses one out-of-fold prediction for each training row. By default, the splitter uses three seeded shuffled folds, stratified jointly by treatment and outcome where supported. Sparse joint strata fall back to treatment stratification, and sparse arms fall back to a shuffled unstratified split. With repeated unit identifiers, group-aware splitters keep every unit in one held-out fold. Group codes are seeded before the unstratified size-balancing fallback. Too few groups, invalid identifiers, overlap and incomplete coverage fail explicitly. A single-class fold uses a constant outcome probability and records a fallback.

Each outcome model gets its own fitted preprocessing. Fold training and holdout indices come from the training partition. The utility is handed only training rows and keeps validation and test out of nuisance fitting and held-out scoring. Diagnostics record fold sizes, arm/event counts, fold fit timings, coverage, a deterministic membership fingerprint, pooled out-of-fold ROC-AUC/AP/log loss/Brier, propensity bounds and residual summaries. The probability fitting callable follows the existing binary outcome model contract.

Let `y_res = Y − m_hat(X)` and `t_res = T − e(X)`. R-learning regresses `y_res / t_res` on features with weights `t_res²`. For each row the weighted squared error equals `(y_res − tau(X) × t_res)²`. We divide weights by their mean to keep weight scale comparable across designs. This rescales the data loss and affects its strength relative to regularization. Ridge uses alpha 1 and LightGBM uses the fixed regularized tree configuration. We reject residual magnitudes below 1e−8 without dropping rows, clipping denominators or silently changing the objective.

On validation, m(X) is the mean of the already-fitted training-fold models. Validation R-loss is the mean squared residual expression with those frozen nuisances. The constant effect reference in each diagnostics row uses the same nuisance predictions, enabling a within-row comparison. Comparing losses across configurations with different nuisance models requires care. Cross-fitting reduces in-sample nuisance overfit and does not by itself fix poor overlap, misspecification or confounding.

Fit uses known synthetic assignment probabilities or Criteo’s released **training** assignment share. Uplift metrics use exactly Session 05’s **validation** propensity convention. Both Criteo shares are benchmark approximations. [Uplift evaluation](UPLIFT_EVALUATION.md) · [R-learner paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.04912)
