# Double machine learning

Partially linear DML estimates a constant structural coefficient. With out-of-fold outcome residual rY = Y − m(X) and treatment residual rT = T − e(X), it solves the orthogonal score equation mean(rT × (rY − theta × rT)) = 0. The solution is sum(rT × rY) / sum(rT²). The empirical derivative is −mean(rT²), and the independent-row sandwich standard error is sqrt(mean(score²)/n) divided by mean(rT²). Near-zero residual treatment variation is rejected. Normal 95% intervals assume suitable nuisance rates, overlap, identification and independent observations. They are asymptotic, can be imperfect in small samples and account for neither missing Criteo cluster identifiers nor the unknown original trial propensities.

Under randomized constant propensity the population coefficient targets the average treatment effect. With heterogeneous effects and varying propensity, the partially linear projection generally weights conditional effects by e(X)(1−e(X)) and need not equal ordinary ATE. The controlled continuous demonstration has a constant structural effect, which avoids that estimand ambiguity.

The binary benchmark cross-fits a logistic outcome nuisance using Session 06 folds and supplies known design probabilities on randomized synthetic data. Criteo uses the released training share as an approximation. LinearDML fits a heterogeneous effect that is linear in the allowed covariates, with an intercept. EconML fits binary logistic outcome nuisances and uses a cloneable constant-probability treatment classifier in three training-only folds. The estimator produces effect estimates and pointwise intervals on the original outcome scale.

## Prespecified high-dimensional demonstration

For each repetition, draw 30 independent standard-normal baseline covariates. Define nonlinear terms `x0²−1`, `x1×x2`, and `sin(x3)`. Assignment has probability `0.10 + 0.80 × sigmoid(0.85(x0²−1) + 0.85x1x2 + 0.40sin(x3) + 0.40x4)`. The outcome combines those nonlinear terms with coefficients 0.90, 0.90 and 0.45, linear terms 0.50x4 and 0.15x5, independent unit normal noise, and **1.25 × assignment**. The remaining 24 covariates are irrelevant. True overlap is bounded by design.

Thirty independent repetitions of 2,400 units compare the naive difference (Welch intervals), linear adjustment on raw covariates (HC1 normal intervals), and DML with cross-fitted LightGBM outcome and propensity nuisances (55 trees, three folds). The linear model deliberately uses conventional raw-covariate adjustment, with no engineered terms. The DML benchmark estimates propensity and reserves the design value for evaluation diagnostics. Report bias, RMSE and observed coverage. Thirty draws offer only a coarse coverage check and this design cannot establish universal superiority.
