# Profit-aware treatment optimization

Expected incremental contribution is estimated CATE times contribution per conversion. Subtract fixed treatment cost to obtain expected incremental profit. Break-even uplift is cost divided by positive contribution. Zero contribution has no finite reported break-even. ROI is incremental profit divided by realized campaign spending, and is undefined at zero spend. The modeled profit and the simulator's true expected profit are expectations. Neither is realized revenue.

The benchmark uses A, B, D and E validation rows and a separate F research-only demonstration. It reloads prespecified Session 05 S-LightGBM, Session 06 X-linear and the Session 04 pooled conversion model. Provenance checks cover training fingerprints and ordered feature contracts. Evaluation checks unique validation identifiers, split labels and isolation from training. A and F also match the Session 10 partition fingerprints. Synthetic truth is loaded only after learned actions are selected, and aligned by unit ID for evaluation and the explicit oracle. F uses the previously reported, unsupported aggregate AIPW as a constant hypothetical score. The status stays `not_identified` regardless of apparent profit. No Criteo monetary results are inferred.

Prespecified simulated contribution and delivery prices are 45 and 2 for uniform economics, discrete-uniform contribution 20–70 and cost 2 for the variable-value design, contribution 20–70 and independent cost 1–7 for the variable-cost design, and contribution 5 and cost 20 for the unfavorable design. Eligibility is an independent seeded 90% draw. Capacity is floor(30% of the comparison population). Budget is floor(2 × population × each of 5%, 10% and 20%). Monetary inputs are represented to the cent. The variable-cost comparison uses a deterministic 180-row subset shared by all policies in that cohort and budget. Full validation and bounded results are separate comparisons.

Random, predictive and uplift ranking scan their fixed order, skipping anything unaffordable or ineligible. They can select negative estimated-profit opportunities. The constant-effect policy ranks its own expected profit using the training arm contrast. Profit optimizers exclude computed zero and negative profit, allow unused budget and capacity, and keep the no-action option. Uniform costs allow exact positive-profit top-K. Variable-cost comparisons include greedy profit and profit-to-cost scans and a SciPy HiGHS MILP with a one-second limit and zero requested relative gap. Positive-profit free opportunities rank first for the ratio heuristic. Equal ranking scores use a seeded shuffle. MILP results retain solver status, bound, gap and runtime. All returned actions are independently checked against exact cents, capacity and eligibility. Regret is reported only against a proven oracle optimum for that same population and constraint set. [SciPy MILP API](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.milp.html)

Outcome-dependent cost is separate. The example pays a discount for every treated conversion, so expected cost is `mu1 × discount`. Contribution value is before incentives. A separate redemption event would require an additional probability model. The illustrative probabilities are fixed. The multi-treatment example uses two hypothetical scoring functions and separately specified valid potential outcome probabilities to evaluate the decisions. At most one nonzero action can be chosen per customer. It fits no multi-treatment causal models.
